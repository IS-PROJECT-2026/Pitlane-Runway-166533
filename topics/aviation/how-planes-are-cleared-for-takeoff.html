AVIATION / AIR TRAFFIC CONTROL

How Planes Are Cleared for Takeoff

Before an aircraft can accelerate down a runway and leave the ground, several things have to happen first. The aircraft must be prepared, the crew must communicate with air traffic control, and the aircraft must be positioned and cleared to depart.

Being ready to take off is not the same as being cleared for takeoff. Air traffic control coordinates aircraft using the airport and considers the traffic situation before issuing the appropriate instructions. This coordination helps aircraft move safely and efficiently through the airport environment.

01 / PREPARATION

Before the aircraft reaches the runway

The takeoff process begins well before the aircraft reaches the runway. The flight crew prepares the aircraft for departure and communicates with the appropriate airport control services.

Depending on the airport and flight, the crew may receive departure and taxi information before moving from its parking position. The aircraft then follows its assigned taxi route toward the departure runway.

During this stage, the aircraft is moving on the airport surface, rather than performing the takeoff itself.

02 / ATC

Who controls the aircraft?

Air traffic control, or ATC, helps coordinate aircraft movements so that traffic can move safely and efficiently. Different control functions may be involved at different stages of an aircraft's movement.

Ground Control

Ground control handles applicable aircraft and vehicle movements on the airport surface. This includes coordinating taxi movements and helping aircraft reach the appropriate departure area.

Tower / Local Control

At tower-controlled airports, the tower is involved in managing runway operations, including takeoffs and landings. Before an aircraft receives a takeoff clearance, the controller must coordinate the departure with the traffic situation.

The Pilot

ATC provides clearances and instructions, but the pilot remains responsible for the safe operation of the aircraft. If an instruction is unclear or appears unsafe, the pilot must resolve the situation with ATC rather than simply proceeding.

03 / HOLDING POINT

What is a holding point?

A holding point is a designated position where an aircraft may wait before entering a runway. It is normally located at the end of a taxiway, just before the runway.

The aircraft may be instructed to hold short of the runway until it receives further instructions from ATC. This helps ensure that the runway is clear and that the departure can be coordinated with other traffic.

04 / THE RUNWAY

What happens at the runway?

Reaching the runway does not automatically mean that an aircraft can begin its takeoff roll. The aircraft may first be instructed to hold short of the runway, or it may receive an instruction to enter the runway and wait.

This is where the distinction between an aircraft being in position and being cleared for takeoff becomes important.

Ready does not mean cleared

A pilot can be completely ready to depart while still waiting for the appropriate takeoff clearance.

05 / LINE UP AND WAIT

Why might a plane enter the runway and still wait?

One instruction a pilot may receive is “Line up and wait.”

This means the aircraft is instructed to enter the departure runway, align itself for departure and wait for further instructions. It is used when the aircraft is being positioned for an imminent departure but a takeoff clearance cannot yet be issued.

Important distinction

Line up and wait is NOT takeoff clearance.

The aircraft must wait for the appropriate takeoff clearance before beginning its takeoff roll.

06 / CLEARANCE

What does “cleared for takeoff” mean?

A takeoff clearance is the authorization that allows the aircraft to begin its departure from the runway.

In FAA terminology, the takeoff clearance identifies the runway and states that the aircraft is cleared for takeoff. The exact radio communication varies according to the situation and operating environment.

Example phraseology

“Runway 27, cleared for takeoff.”

Once the pilot has the appropriate clearance and has completed the necessary checks, the aircraft can begin its takeoff roll.

07 / THE COMPLETE SEQUENCE

Putting it all together

The process can be simplified into a sequence of coordinated steps:

  1. The aircraft is prepared for departure.
  2. The crew communicates with the appropriate ATC service.
  3. The aircraft receives taxi instructions.
  4. The aircraft taxis toward its assigned runway.
  5. The aircraft waits at the appropriate position when instructed.
  6. The aircraft may be instructed to line up and wait.
  7. ATC issues the takeoff clearance when appropriate.
  8. The aircraft begins its takeoff roll.

What looks like a simple moment from the passenger cabin is therefore the result of coordination between the flight crew, air traffic control and the airport environment.

GLOSSARY

Key terms

ATC
Air Traffic Control — services that help manage aircraft movements and the flow of air traffic.
Runway
A defined area prepared for aircraft takeoff and landing.
Taxiway
A route used by aircraft to move around an airport between runways and other areas.
Holding Point
A designated position where an aircraft may wait before entering a runway.
Line Up and Wait
An ATC instruction to enter the runway, align for departure and wait. It is not authorization to take off.
Takeoff Clearance
ATC authorization for an aircraft to begin its takeoff.
